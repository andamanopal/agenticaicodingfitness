Coordinate Frames
Section 05
Everything Lives in a Frame
A position means nothing until you say “relative to what.” Every joint, camera, and object reports numbers in some frame, and a rigid transform is the dictionary that rewrites one frame’s numbers in another. Get the direction of that dictionary wrong and the arm reaches the mirror image of where you meant.
The Frames in This Workshop and the Transforms Between Them

A Pose Is Position + Orientation, Packed into One 4×4

The rigid transformConcept
T_a_b = | R (3×3)   t (3×1) |    R = orientation of frame b, seen from a
        | 0  0  0      1    |    t = origin of frame b, seen from a

p_a      = T_a_b · p_b          # rewrite a point from frame b into frame a
T_a_c    = T_a_b · T_b_c        # chain: a←b and b←c compose into a←c
T_b_a    = inverse(T_a_b)       # flip the arrow’s direction
You read…As…
T_base_cameraThe camera’s pose expressed in the base frame — subscripts read right-to-left: base ← camera.
T_a_b · p_bA point known in frame b, converted into frame a.
inverse(T_a_b)The same relationship read the other way, T_b_a. Direction is not optional.
Every later stage meets in the base frame: IK targets, reconstructed objects, and placement destinations are all expressed there. That is why physical calibration (Runbook C) has one core job—pin down T_base_camera—and why the multi-pose check verifies a single fixed T_gripper_camera. Once those are known, the chain above turns camera pixels into base-frame geometry the arm can reach.

Kinematics
Section 06
From a Point in Space to Joint Angles
Forward kinematics maps one joint configuration to one gripper pose. Inverse kinematics searches backward from a desired pose and may find multiple configurations, one configuration, or no reachable configuration.
One Forward Answer, Multiple Inverse Answers

How This Repository Chooses One

StepLibrary and functionWhat it contributes
1 · Seedsolve_ik(..., q_init=seed)Three starting postures explore different local IK branches.
2 · Observemujoco.mj_kinematics
mujoco.mj_comPos		Forward kinematics reveals the current gripper pose and target error.
3 · Correctmujoco.mj_jacSite
numpy.linalg.solve		The Jacobian and damped solve produce Δq; numpy.clip enforces joint limits.
4 · Selectmin(solutions, key=lambda result: result[1])Smallest position error wins; results above 30 mm or with an invalid wrist are rejected.
What are we using? No dedicated IK package. Our custom solve_ik() combines MuJoCo’s kinematics and Jacobians with NumPy linear algebra. It is seed-dependent and does not globally optimize travel, collisions, manipulability, or clearance.

Camera Model
Section 09
How a Camera Turns 3D into Pixels
The wrist camera is the only sensor. To use its pixels as geometry you need two things—how it projects the world onto the sensor (intrinsics) and where it sits (extrinsics)—and you must accept one hard limit: a single photograph carries no absolute scale.
Projection, and the Depth It Throws Away

Two Sets of Numbers Make a Camera a Sensor

PieceSymbolWhat it fixes
IntrinsicsK = fx, fy, cx, cy (+ distortion)How a 3D ray maps to a pixel—focal length and image center; distortion undoes lens curvature.
ExtrinsicsT_base_cameraWhere the camera sits in the base frame—a rigid transform, exactly as on the frames page.
The projectionConcept
pixel (u,v)  ←  K · [ R | t ] · P_world       intrinsics × extrinsics × 3D point
                     └ extrinsics ┘
# calibration solves K (intrinsics, ≤ 2 px RMS) and T_base_camera (pose, ≤ 1.5 px RMSE)

Why One Photo Cannot Give Metric Depth

A pixel names a ray, not a point. Everything along that ray—a pea up close, a beach ball far away—lands on the same pixel. So one RGB image fixes direction but never distance: monocular depth is relative, not metric, on its own.

The workshop recovers real meters with three anchors: (1) the known table plane at z = 0 gives an absolute reference the learned depth is affine-fit to; (2) Depth Anything V2 supplies the dense relative depth to fit; (3) the three overlapping survey views triangulate, and only points ≥ 2 views agree on survive. This is also why calibration quality is scored in reprojection error—a wrong K or pose warps every downstream coordinate.

Inside the Agent
Section 16
Tools, Schemas, and the Coordinate Firewall
The previous section said the model “picks validated tools” and never touches the actuators. This is the machinery that makes that literally true: a typed tool schema, structured-output validation, and a firewall that keeps raw coordinates out of the language model’s hands entirely.
One Agent Turn — and Where Coordinates Are Allowed to Live

The Model Emits a Typed Tool Call, Not Prose

Each turn the model outputs one structured tool call—a name plus arguments—checked against a schema before anything runs. Wrong shape, unknown field, or bad type: rejected, and it tries again. The schema is the contract; free-form text never reaches the robot.

Symbolic in, geometry outConcept
find_objects(query="writing tool")     → ["object_3"]      # ids, not coordinates
plan(target_id="object_3",
     destination="right side")         → validated plan     # symbolic in, geometry out
# No manipulation tool accepts an XYZ from the model—so there is nowhere to put one.
The language model ownsDeterministic code owns
Interpreting intent, choosing the next tool, filling symbolic arguments, asking to clarify, proposing labels.Every coordinate, IK, collision check, confidence gate, the schema validator, and all actuator commands.
This is the concrete enforcement of two earlier rules: the roadmap’s “the LLM may choose the next tool, but it cannot invent the missing evidence,” and the agentic loop’s responsibility boundary. Because the manipulation tools accept only symbolic references—an object_id that find_objects actually returned, a named destination—a hallucinated number has no parameter to enter. The firewall is structural, not a matter of the model behaving.

Earn the Right to Touch Hardware

One Agent Contract, Two Execution Environments

Before Hardware: Acceptance Checklist

  • No truth leakage in estimated mode
  • At least 50 randomized episodes
  • Supported tasks succeed ≥ 90%
  • Exact demo succeeds 20 times
  • Zero forbidden collisions
  • Failures detected by verification
  • Every run is replayable
  • Plan requires human approval
Physical gate: the dashboard requires a camera-specific profile, registered base/table coordinates, three distinct measured survey poses, calibrated limits, manual joint-sign confirmation, a passing multi-pose kinematic check, and a separately approved exact plan. Joint-sequence completion is still unverified until a new observation checks the object.

Physical Safety Rules

Control the workspace
Light objects only. No faces, fingers, liquids, valuables, sharp items, or spectators inside reach.
Control authority
Low speed, bounded workspace, accessible stop, one operator, and one approved plan at a time.
Expect mismatch
Calibration, friction, backlash, and camera mounting make sim-to-real an experiment—not a copy operation.
Emergency principle: a human stop always overrides the agent. The language model never owns unrestricted actuator access.

The Reality Gap
Section 19
Where Simulation Stops and Reality Begins
A passing simulation proves the plan is geometrically valid in a perfectly known world. Hardware is neither perfectly known nor perfectly obedient. This page names the gaps a green simulation cannot see—so you expect them instead of discovering them mid-motion.
Motion Runs Open-Loop — Unmodeled Effects Accumulate
Gap sourceWhy the simulator can’t see itThis workshop’s response
Actuation & mechanics
backlash, friction, gravity sag, servo lag		Sim uses idealized position actuators; real Feetech servos chase goals with slack and delay.Low speed, calibrated joint limits, and a per-joint tracking tolerance (~1.5°) that aborts on a miss.
Sensing & perception
no depth sensor, lighting shift, mount & calibration error, motion blur		Sim renders perfect frames with exact, known camera poses.Per-configuration calibration, reprojection-error gates, ≥ 2-view agreement, and settle-before-capture.
Contact & grasp
unknown friction, object give, slip, contact forces		The contact solver is an approximation, and the arm has no force or slip sensor.Light rigid objects, top-down grasps, and treating completion as executed_unverified until rescanned.
World & safety
cables, clamps, people, latency, no certified e-stop		None of these live in the model at all.One human approval per action, one operator at the stop, a bounded workspace, motor power kept reachable.
Every response above is the same four moves: measure instead of assume (calibration), gate every motion (approvals), move slowly, and verify after the fact. Sim-to-real is an experiment, not a copy operation—a green simulation earns you the right to run the experiment, not a guarantee of its result.

Closing the Gap — Where These Challenges Become Extensions

Sense contact
Add tactile or current-based force feedback to detect a real grasp and catch slip before the lift.
Close the loop
Visual servoing corrects the trajectory mid-motion instead of trusting one open-loop plan.
Shrink the model gap
Domain randomization and system identification make the simulator predict the real arm more faithfully.

First Recovery Step

Viewer will not open
macOS passive-viewer scripts need mjpython, not python—or pass --headless to skip the window. If mjpython aborts with a libpython3.11.dylib load error (common on uv-built virtualenvs), prefix it with DYLD_FALLBACK_LIBRARY_PATH="$(python -c 'import sysconfig;print(sysconfig.get_config_var("LIBDIR"))')".
IK residual is high
Check target reachability and try a bent-arm or pan-aligned seed.
Wrong object selected
Inspect evidence crops and aliases; ask the user to disambiguate.
Grasp fails unexpectedly
Run oracle mode, inspect pose error, friction, jaw clearance, and verification output.
The workshop loop: observe → ground → plan → simulate → approve → execute → verify. When uncertain, move backward in the loop rather than guessing forward.
